I [voted](https://apnews.com/article/taiwan-local-elections-dpp-kmt-china-cbfaf36781893dc1a7830b702889f829)!

Grandma (94) let everyone know she also voted, giving us the [finger(s)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r2SkuwEcTpo). My cousin's family gave her a ride earlier that morning to Dad's childhood neighborhood, whereas I commuted back to *my* childhood neighborhood to cast 卜s for the:

1. Mayor of Taipei
2. Taipei City Councilors
3. ~~Borough Chief~~
4. National Referendum: deciding whether to abolish *Nuclear-Free Homeland*.

By the time I arrived at the classroom and received ballots in white, pink, and yellow, few were still in line. On the news, people had lined up early in small towns, and ferries were packed heading to offshore islands. Spirits were high in the city, and many small businesses I passed had orange banners and supporters roaming around. It was hard not wanting to be involved.

### Two Weeks Ago at a Chopstick Shop

**Man in turquoise tutus:** Would you also like a bag?

**Me:** A bag?

**Man in turquoise tutus:**  The ribbon. It's free.

My cousins had attended a couple of his live campaigns in front of temples and on the streets, where groups of all ages followed and cheered enthusiastically. His toothy smile and twin crescents were all over buildings and moving buses. A young but foreign face. I personally knew three friends who’d flown back from abroad to fulfill their civic duties. What a feat, and a shame for those who could've easily done so in 30min [¹](#fn1).

Next to my name on the registry was my uncle's, who had already voted, and my cousin's wife, who's behind me with her signature confused look. All I needed was my ID and chop (fingerprint works too) to vote. I stood in front of the election bulletin for a final look at the candidates' policies and numbers before stepping into my booth. It felt good to know I had a say in all this.


<div class="botanical-divider">・・・・・</div>

At lunch, Grandma was visibly giddy (over the [dishes](https://share.google/Evb2Da0MLAcFI3se0)) and talkative. She dropped two explosive revelations: one we verified with the party involved's mom (my aunt) after a quick call, and the other, we pretended not to speculate about further.

> "The two paid a visit last evening to give me three bunches of muscat grapes."

Grandma asked in her raspy voice whether mine was coming soon too. I wasn't sure she meant the ride or the ticket. She then casually added, *never regret it once vowing for eternity.*
