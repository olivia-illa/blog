> Rick: You know, I love standing like this? I pretend I'm an eagle or an owl. Any kind of bird. Do you consider an owl a bird?
>
> Frances: Rick, please stand back. That glass is not reinforced.



It is the perfect allegory for the illusion of freedom. Having finally purchased the skyscraper penthouse of his dreams, Rick finds it entirely hollow with no furniture, no personal belongings, no loved one to share it with. Trapped in his role, he's physically incapable of stopping his hunt for the next monetization scheme, even if somewhere deep down he longs to fly away from the misery. 

Imagine Rick dying the exact same way Frances' mom did: trying to be a bird. Except he would never reach for anything beyond the system’s construction. He stays safe behind those ~~sealed~~ glass windows, unlike the open ones Frances' mom constantly longed for, ultimately climbing out to her death. 

In this dark comedy, some humans will never escape the system that has trapped them so deeply. Every character is flawed, blinded by what they believe to be the truth or by what the blue mushroom symbolizes as morality. For Rick, that means profiting off whatever the miracle mushroom can do, even if it requires selling synthetic versions stamped with Frances' face, or peddling it as a cool new funky chip flavor. For a brief second, he pretends he actually wants to save the world, but really he is just terrified of the legal consequences of distributing an untested drug.

Rick is a classic portrayal of a businessman who cares about nothing beyond turning a profit—a single-minded pursuit that becomes deeply comical once reduced to its barest form. Anything can be sold. 

Even if there comes a moment where he’s finally had enough and wishes to escape the endless cycle of chasing the next big product, the glass will always catch him. He is a piece of the machine, valuable only until he isn't.
