Wow, I'm considered the *older generations*... Truth be told, Mom, gran and I used to rewatched Devil Wears Prada  after dinner when I stayed over in the summer. And somehow I'd always thought Mom was into Sex and City back in the days. 


> The Earles of the world likely watched cultural influencers before them take hard-line political stances to gain audience support, only to be forced to walk back their statements when the climate had shifted. “As soon as you start to stand for things, you will be held to a totally different standard than if you just kind of are a canvas on which people can project a kind of bland positivity,” Burke said.


<br> 

Perhaps her audience has shifted their content preferences, not because girl bosses are dead, but because women’s choices (whether categorized as trad, feminist, workaholic, or mother) get criticized under the patriarchy either way. I’m now far more interested in understanding how we developed this skewed system from a structural standpoint, looking at how historians, commentators on legal and economy view it. 

Explain how I should navigate this world, please. 

We have to look past the superficial suffering. Often Alex and her guests struggles aren't comparable to us in any level. What most are getting out are background noise and perhaps 5sec of encouragement. And not to devalue sharing personal experiences, but we need to be out there living life rather than tuning in to others. We need actual self-realization.

There's limited time for podcasts, and I prioritize ones that keep me informed for my next move. I’ve spent the past decade learning my emotional patterns, tendencies, and coming to self-preservation. *Call Her Daddy* played a role in that chapter, but it’s time to move forward. Don't worry, I'll tune back in only when I'm stumped and need a quick boost of girl power.
