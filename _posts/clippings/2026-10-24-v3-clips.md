> None of this was fame or charisma. All of it was structure, and structure is learnable.

1. She answered in one sentence, then expanded. Every single time. The answer came first, the reasoning after. 

2. She paused before answering, never during. A beat before she started. Nothing in the middle. Pausing before reads as thinking. Pausing halfway through reads as losing it.

3. She used numbers instead of “a lot.” “Eleven markets.” “Two years.” Specific words make you sound like you’ve been paying attention, because you have.

4. She said “I don’t know” without a change in her voice. Same tone, same pace, no flinch. Then a date. It made everything else she said more believable, not less.

5. She never repeated the question back. Most people do it to buy time, and it costs them the first four seconds of every answer. She just answered.

6. She used one comparison per point. Not three. One good analogy and then she moved on. Over-explaining and being clear are opposites more often than people think.

7. She stopped when she was finished. No trailing sentence. No looking around for a sign it had landed. The silence after her answer belonged to her, not to her doubt.


Do not over-explain yourself, noted.
