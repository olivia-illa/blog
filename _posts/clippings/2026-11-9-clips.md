> It's easy to assume that others think the worst of you. But when you assume what others think, maybe all you're doing is turning them into a character that only exists in your mind. The nice thing is, sometimes all it takes is a change in perspective to make the world feel brand-new.

We overthink others perception of us which hinders our actions.


## The Fielder Method

> Okay. And what's the best way to fill in the form?
>
> Just do the action.

Nathan says in the beginning of this episode, that one should just do it. It's easier for him to say so as an instructor, which makes me wonder if he's also playing a character here.


## Infinite Loops of Understanding (until Nathan looses the plot)

> Reliving the day again as Thomas made me realize there was a whole other layer to his experience I hadn't considered.

Nathan steps into Thomas's shoes as an actor student, as Thomas stalking his primary, as Thomas imitating Thomas as his primary, as Thomas being forced out of his own apartment to live in a simulated version of his primary's shared space, and finally, as Thomas living in his own apartment.


> But no matter how deep I went, there were still parts of Thomas that were a mystery to me.
> 
> But maybe that's as close as you can get. And that last step in understanding someone is always just a guess.


Nathan's comedic storytelling is a magnified example of how far we are willing to step into someone's shoes to understand their situation. It's an exercise that allows more empathy and less self-consciousness when faced with problems.
