I'd rather be with a bear right now. He’s built like one, at least 5'11", and talks too much. I'm holding my breath, trying to seem invisible in a 400 sq ft space.

The painter’s wearing a blue collared shirt and camouflage pants splattered with varying shades of white paint. He walked in carrying a stained Costco bag (worker-chic), a large cardboard box holding a putty knife, spackle, painter's tape, and plastic sheeting, plus two paint buckets: one for water and the other with beige paint. He drinks straight from a giant jug; the first I’ve seen bring his own water bottle out of all the contractors who’ve attempted (and failed) to fix this windowsill leak.

He has been squatting next to my desk for five minutes, watching TikTok on mute after scraping loose paint off for ten.

I’m guessing he is in his early 40s, maybe with a young kid. He’s tan-skinned, has a slight belly, and is clean-shaven. He watches videos with thick, furrowed eyebrows (the same when he works on the walls), a focused expression, and a somewhat warm smile and eyes when he breaks into small talk. He presumably gets aggressive if poked the wrong way, so I'm afraid to tell him not to lean against the couch arm while he's watching TikTok.

Ah, right, his side profile looks like Robert De Niro. That's it. But he's missing a mole. The menacingly cheerful grandpa in *Meet the Parents*, who could just as easily switch to the ruthless hitman in *The Irishman*.

> *"I rang the bell thrice, and no one came, so I thought I'd just walk in."*

Our first encounter just so happens to be grabbing the doorknob at the exact same time from opposite sides, jolting a yelp out of me.

Thirty minutes have passed. The dents are patched and covered with fresh paint, and he's back to scrolling. He’s remarkably focused, doesn’t bounce between other apps. I can't tell what he's watching, though I wouldn't be surprised if it's AI drama. Why did the landlord tell me he needs four hours to work on this wall? It's been an hour, and he's practically done.

> *"Can I get a fan to blow on the walls?"*

Oh, I figured it out. He's waiting for the joint compound and paint to dry between coats. He's pretty thorough, touching up little spots and sanding, or smoothing out imperfections. He's brushed the final bits of paint off the trim, pulled off the plastic sheeting, and gone back to his comfy spot scrolling.

Now he's pulled out a caulk gun to seal the seams along the baseboards and corners, then brushes a coat of primer over the wall and the wood flooring (huh, did the landlord ask for that too?). His steps are heavy when he walks across the room to rinse the towels for cleaning up the paint and dust on the floor. He’s chugging water again. I should be drinking mine too. 

> *"Looks a lot better now, huh? It’s been a while since I was here."*

> *(Takes photos of his work from different angles.)*


Admittedly, it is quite satisfying watching the paintbrush stroke over the once rusty, moldy wall. There's barely any discoloration where the old and new paint meet, almost as if a white chocolate glaze were poured over it. I can still see the wet spots around the corners, which he carefully reminded me to set dry until evening. 

I guess I'm fine being around men who know their craft, though I don't recognize him.

Previously, d and I joked that our apartment looked like a mob shootout, with bullet holes punched all over. They were drainage holes left by the last worker who finally figured out how to let trapped water out from behind the damaged wall. At long last, after months of cleaning up mini floods and fallen paint, we have a smooth wall.
