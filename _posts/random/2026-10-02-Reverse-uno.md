Over time, I’ve heard comments from those returning home after graduation that made me question whether they were joking, exaggerating, or unintentionally causing second-hand embarrassment. I decided to make a list. 

Mostly because I wonder if I've ever accidentally projected that same tone-deafness, something my parents worked hard to prevent by ensuring my brother and I fully absorbed both ends of the cultures we grew up in.

* "My fingers have gradually forgotten how chopsticks work." — From someone who eats at home 70% of the time.

* "Using your phone in public is overrated; no one should be scrolling while walking." — Concluded while comparing cities with phone-snatchers and pickpockets to the streets here, or say Tokyo.

* "Picnic culture only exists in places where people treat parks like beaches." — You’ve not witnessed what t and I  have in the parks here.

* "Dairy milk is unhealthy; non-dairy options like oat or almond milk are superior." — Meanwhile, soy milk has never felt trendier.

* "Thrifting is so cool." — Interesting, considering people called me cheap when I did it here back in high school.

* "I don’t care how much people stare at me here when I dress revealingly." — People will always stare if you have great style.

* "There’s just so much culture—music and art—in this city. I’m going to have trouble finding like-minded friends if I go home." — Genuinely felt too uncultured to even comment.

* "Walking home at midnight never feels safe, not even back home." — I don’t think you’ve ever actually hung out past 12 here, not to mention living in the heart of a city where the streetlights are never off.

* "There are subway stations every couple of blocks! Public transit is just so much more convenient and runs all night." — There are also YouBike stations on every street corner operating 24/7.

* "Books are so much cheaper over there." — Get a free Eslite membership; I swear reading one book a month is already way above average.

* "I can’t bake or cook here." — As if baking was ever a genuine interest.

* "I don’t understand why my hometown friends claim I barely hang out with them; I just came out for hot pot!" — Well, it’s not hard to see through the distancing.

* "It's hard to travel around, or go to another country on a whim." — Always makes me chuckle coming from those who've never explored even the tips of this island.

* "There is no beauty in this city or pretty boys." — To that, I'd have to agree, only partially.

* "The simplest happiness comes from getting blueberries from the farmers market and eating them on the bench." - We get them in bulk from Costco, does that count?

* "The weather's wet outside, but I am dry inside." - Hard to tell if it was in a literal sense or lack of love life in this humidity. I followed up with a joke, but realized it was




All jokes aside, I do sympathize with these comments—the performative effort to wear a specific identity like a wolf skin, or to cling to a place merely visited or briefly lived in. I certainly have done the same, intentionally or not.

But having lived in one city for the majority of my life, somewhere some say a complete opposite of my persona, language, and cultural interests, strangers sometimes still question whether I am an alien. I always answer no, and they know it’s true based on how much I appreciate my surroundings, the way I live and interact.

You can’t escape the roots that shaped you, so why not embrace them in a way you naturally would, even if things have  changed on the surface.
