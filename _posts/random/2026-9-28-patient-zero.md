Post-watch reviews (after figuring out I'd turned off the video play function on Spotify🤦‍♀):

- All the cups and glasses being flipped off like what cats do, got me so confused, until I watched for the second time to realize it's a warning from the Taylor, the ex wife.
- Concrete walls like prisoner, caged bird, kept out from his cold cold heart.
- The mirror does look like The 1975 frame; rarely are mirrors so narrow, and placed in portrait in huge mansions.
- I wonder what the swimming meant? When all you'd hear is your own voice? Drowning in self-doubt, then surfacing with epiphany.
- My favorite shot is when Taylor shoves the door open backwards, breaking the fourth wall, then bringing us back into the story. And when Colin's character goes, "I know."
- Doors doors doors, where are they leading to?
- Dakota and Taylor looked gorgeous in sparkly, barely clinging slip gowns. Fits Dakota's typical aesthetic--a seductive vengeance.
- Surprised by Cara Delevingne playing the other woman. All the mischievous sensual aura we need.
- Why is the Dakota the drinker? Taylor's not. And to the point of vomiting? Or is she disgusted by the revelation?
- The last time Taylor was on the roof of a car, she survived a crash in *Bad Blood*; Cara was in that MV too. And her new beachy shoulder-length hair at the VMAS? Is she hinting Rep TV?? Also this comment when I revisited it ["WHY DID I NOT REALIZE HOW ICONIC THIS WAS WHEN I WAS YOUNGER?!?!"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QcIy9NiNbmo&list=RDQcIy9NiNbmo&start_radio=1)
- Love how Taylor spells "run" with the spillage. I've always wondered in the promo why she's pointing at blood??
- 1986-2022 apparently a clue to Verity (2026), the movie Dakota stars in. But why the free promo, unless the music will be featured?
- Who does Dakota bring the second rose to? Mourning her future self?





Definitely TTPD coded, which brings us Maylors to speculate whether this is a warning song to Gabi, or whoever's the next victim. But why would Taylor want be a savior if ultimately she believes their love story will linger and haunt her, *Still alive, killing time at the cemetery/ Never quite buried*, but also rise against all odds, *We been on a winning streak/ He jokes that it's heroin but this time with an "E"/ Cause the sign on your heart said it's still reserved for me*. 

As honorable Taylor may seem, she is the pettiest lover. Imagine Taylor wanting to potentially save the girl from heartbreak and regret--from the supposed love of HER life? In *Babylon*, the latest release of an obvious Matty song, she sings of his current lover, *Sneaking to the bathroom to call her mom/ And say, "It's not workin'/It's just not worth it if we're not what he wants*. 

I genuinely view *Patient Zero* as Taylor swallowing the sour grapes, pretending to be honorable, a reverse, wicked way to warn Gabi what she's committing to (and if they ever have kids??) will outlast the legendary one she had. Ultimately, Taylor believes in this love *I can fix him, no, really I can (no, really I can)*, by leaving an open door/ question, a barely noticeable thread, a slightest chance, *If one thing had been different/ Would everything be different today?*
