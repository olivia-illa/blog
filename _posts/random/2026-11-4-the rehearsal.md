I approached [*The Rehearsal (2022)*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt10802170/) with no prior knowledge, assuming each episode was a stand-alone, psychological experiment that real participants had signed up for with genuine problems. 

Kor’s stern expression during practice and his underwhelmed reaction toward the 1-to-1 replica of the Alligator Lounge, should’ve been the first clue that both the fake Kor and Kor himself were actors in Nathan’s elaborate storytelling. Nathan himself is starring in a self-directed documentary, playing pretend while grappling with ~~reality's~~ the show’s unpredictable nature.

> Each week, Adam will grow three years, and for each age, multiple child actors will be needed in order to comply with Oregon state labor laws that limit the number of hours a child can work.

In the second episode, Nathan pivots from rehearsing Kor’s confession [¹](#fn1) to simulating Angela’s prolonged delay in having kids. Personally, I’d pay for the experience; it’s a brilliant setup that captures the fears women face before starting a family, such as securing a supportive partner and enough resources for a safe environment.

## Angela Happy Dances

Nathan’s narration here feels like a Freudian slip, implying to the audience (from his gods-eye/ production-CCTV view) that playing house can never truly replicate raising a real child, even as he embraces the simulation's absurd limitations. 

> And I started to wonder if she was less interested in an accurate simulation of her future life and instead just wanted a fun vacation in her dream home.

In this scene, Angela is happy dancing in the kitchen, eating her sandwich after a full night's sleep because the baby actor cannot work past 7 pm. Nathan replaces the baby with a robotic doll that wails according to a monitored real baby via a baby monitor. However, the contractor manning the trigger falls asleep, failing to provide Angela with the full experience. 

*How could you not laugh at Nathan’s brilliance?!*

It’s these meta-commentaries that make me fall in love with Nathan’s storytelling as he grapples with the unfolding events, learning from the gap between anticipated outcomes and participant feedback. The most hilarious visual is Angela harvesting *homegrown* zucchini and peppers from the yard, completely oblivious to the hard work of farming, and the grocery store stickers still attached to them [²](#fn2). 

## Life doesn’t go as planned (Baby Daddy)

> He's bailing, he's leaving. Wasn't cut out for it, I guess. Not everybody is.

There’s no bailing out in real life, especially after committing to raising another human. Though having a co-parent was Angela’s hopeful request, it should have been established as a fixed baseline so she could focus entirely on whether parenting is for her, with or without a baby daddy.

## Fake Kor's Response to nathan’s practice Confession

> I appreciate your motivation...you've tainted, you've ruined the whole thing for me, 'cause I knew the answers, I knew the answers, and I'm on TV now, and everybody now is gonna think that I didn't know them, that I'm a fake and you...

In [another example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQ6aZnyeSpo), Nathan attempts to perfect a rehearsal by confessing that he planted the trivia answers [³](#fn3). While the fake Kor in Nathan's rehearsal reacts with anger to the revelation, Nathan ultimately chickens out of the ~~real-world~~ confession, anticipating a devastating consequence. Instead, he says:

> I think you're... a great guy.
>
> I don't give compliments a lot, so I wanted to come clean about just kind of saying the compliment.

By the end of episode two, we see Nathan adopting the same methods he taught Kor, by reading off a flowchart of all the responses he might receive from the child actors' parents as he notifies them that he'll be participating in the experiment. 

> It's actually great news for you 'cause now, there'll be two people looking after Charlie instead of one. Isn't that exciting?
>
> Yeah, that's great. I'm not sure if I sense any hesitancy in your voice, but if so, it's completely understandable.

By inserting himself directly into the show as the baby daddy, Nathan blurs the boundary between production and reality.

## Patrick's Emotional Response to the Fake Brother

> I think that's what he would want.
>
> So, please. We're brothers, you know, we're supposed to be working together.

In the third episode, a new variable enters the simulation: genuine emotion. Nathan introduces this by orchestrating a narrative layer between Patrick and the fake brother. A play within a play. Patrick wants to confront his brother over suspicions that his girlfriend is after their inherited wealth. This elaborate scheme places them on equal footing, allowing Patrick to vent real frustrations regarding his brother's disapproval in the rehearsals.

## Patrick Lets Things Go 

> I never heard from Patrick again.
>
> Maybe for some, the rehearsal itself is enough.

As much as these participants could’ve simply gone to a good friend or a therapist's office, The Rehearsal remains a fascinating len into human revelations when the burden of unknown events become less daunting and consequential than they'd anticipated. Patrick simply had to let his emotions go; the person across didn't really mattered.
