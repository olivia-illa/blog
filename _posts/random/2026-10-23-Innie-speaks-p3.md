> The Peter principle is a concept in management developed by Laurence J. Peter which observes that people in a hierarchy tend to rise to "a level of respective incompetence": employees are promoted based on their success in previous jobs until they reach a level at which they are no longer competent, as skills in one job do not necessarily translate to another.

-- Definition from [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_principle)

<br>

After hearing about the **Peter Principle**, I have some thoughts.

My last promotion was a year and a half ago. I was squeezed dry handling both daily financial reporting and being on call 16/24 throughout the weekend (due to security and confidentiality, I can’t detail the work)—all without the AI tools we have now, just a chatbot good for SQL. I gave up my passion project, teaching English speaking on Fridays, to absorb every little task my teammates wanted to offload immediately. 

A year and a half later, with half a kidney dead and my nervous system fried, I was rewarded alongside the company-wide salary adjustment. Everyone got extra dough, except I also got an additional title—*senior*—slapped onto mine, though nobody ever explained why. Still, I was pleasantly surprised and received <a href="#fn1-section">congratulatory DMs</a> from my big boss and lead.

When it came to this year’s promotion cycle—initially kept a secret among only a select group—I’d let my big boss know I’d taken on more *important* work, which was true as the company needed to be stringent and compliant under the gov’s new VASP policies. This time, I was hit with a **You haven't reached the next role yet, and s mentioned a few incidents.** Well did he also mention the fact that he’d berated me in that meeting with three other his buddies in what was an interrogation disguised as a brainstorming sesh. On top of that, I got slapped on the wrist with a **Were you not happy with your raise last year?**

I was stunned. I thought promotions were earned once you worked hard and showed up 99% of the time. Meanwhile, the new guy who joined a year ago worked remotely half the week, made jokes, got his without having to prove his worth[¹](#fn1). And because part of my workload has now been delegated to the new hires, meaning I’m no longer visibly fighting back tears at work, management interpreted it as not doing enough, as incompetence for the next role up.
