It will be exactly 28 days, or precisely 696 hours, the longest we’ve been apart since we met. I was taken aback by the stats (as I’ve only ever counted us in years) because mentally, it felt like a week long when I registered the passing time. Let me explain.

Occasionally, d will go on a solo trip or stay out of town for a week to recharge from the having to showing up daily  for us. Regarding this space, the apartment, there are built up resentment that I won't talk about it in this post. Unlike me, he prefers staying away from city, out of sight to feel fully relaxed. I, on the other hand, will spend weekends with my parents to be a real brat, or have rare sleepovers with colleagues to really let loose (and laugh). Our time spent apart almost feel like a retreat rather than a punishment in prison.

<br>

## 28 days = 4 weeks


The first week went by in a blitz, as I booked every single evening with either a run or a catch-up with a friend. 
*“I forgot that you existed/ And I thought that it would kill me, but it didn't,"* I chirped. 28th was coming up and I wanted meet my friends before then.

By the second week, I found down time to journal and update the blog. I spent the weekend having tea and thrifting with c, did some cycling, and searched for my next online obsession, a conspiracy to deep dive into or someone to drool over. 

The third week was when I realized I had completed a full roster of friends, received d’s second phone call, and baked something that took half a day. I also took a mini family road trip down south for Dad's college reunion, speeding down the highway to feel alive and active.

By the fourth week, I spammed d with stickers for attention: *“Do you even miss me?”* I started reading again, and talking to myself more. Tbf, if I were the one traveling, I’d be selfishly immersing myself in the presence, so I don’t blame him. But also it's been four weeks, and I had no clue where or when he's wrapping up.

<br>
