Today was the follow-up appointment, and five concerned family members and friends had already reminded me to update them on the results. (They knew the cheapskate and aloofness in me would've skipped it.) Earlier in the week, I’d pre-checked my blood work on the gov healthcare app (we really do have the best national healthcare system in the world). Among the four test categories and a wall of abbreviated medical terms, I spotted one flagged in red: my white blood cell count, which wasn’t surprising considering my ankle had been swollen. Still, maybe the doc would have a different take.

I’d already gone back to running the day before, doing a quick 6k with j, whom I hadn’t seen since she flew out for the NYC Marathon. She’d finished her first full, and I was proud of her feat and excited to hear about her experience. I also bumped into i, whom I later introduced to d as he'd last minutely come out free-skating with me. My ankle had fully recovered, and I was enjoying the runs even more now that evening temperatures were dropping.

## AT the doctor's appointment that afternoon:

**Doc:** Your white blood cell count was only slightly above the normal threshold of 9,060. Barely a warning sign. Plus the pain in your ankle was causing inflammation, so this was completely normal.

**Me:** How do we explain the severe pain right after eating the lobster?

*Me probing for more.*

**Doc:** You could be overworking your ankles, the marathons, trainings etc., even without an apparent injury. At least from the test results, there’s nothing indicating any underlying abnormality in your health.

*Me squinting skeptically.*

**Doc:** Tell you what—I’ll prescribe you a soothing gel to relief any swelling or burning in the future.

He prescribed the same gel the last time d came in with a bump and throbbing pain in his right thumb (after taking an X-ray and finding no plausible explanation beyond overuse). My intuition was right. I'd paid $10 for a tube of gel I already had at home with no further findings what was wrong. 

I biked home, beating the MRT by 4min, speeding through the lights like I feet was on lava. Honestly, I wished the doctor had just told me there was no need to be back and waste medical resources.

Dad later texted and said I had paid for a peace of mind and that I should be grateful.
