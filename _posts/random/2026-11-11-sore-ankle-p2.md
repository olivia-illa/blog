I heard a tiny, high-pitched pop. Air escaped narrowly, then another, and another, each ten seconds apart. Four vials were collected—unlike Elizabeth’s miracle single drop. There were a few possibilities the doctor wanted to check.

Earlier that day at the hospital, I'd explained my symptoms to the doc and how I’d self-diagnosed with arthritis—*the disease of kings*. Ashamed yet confident, I expected him to nod agreeably.

> *“You know, it’s highly unlikely at your age to have arthritis, let alone gout. It’s mostly men in their late 50s who get it.”*

True. I keep a well-balanced diet, living off the meats my parents deliver over the weekend alongside the fish and squid Dad catches. It’s certainly a privilege, if not taking full advantage of living nearby. The truth is, who else is going to help finish the chicken breasts and beef cuts they buy in bulk at Costco? Plus, most evenings I cook for two and pack meals for lunch. I’m the type to be proud of a full fridge and a well-stocked pantry.

> *Doc, last night was an exception. Lobster’s not usually on my menu. Nothing's been out of the ordinary in terms of my diet lately. I was even jogging last week.*

Going by my symptoms, and after confirming my instincts with G, it had to be gout. Last evening when we got home, as I tried taking off my right shoe with my left foot—like usual, the lazy way—I was stuck.

> ***AHHH HELPPP!! I CAN'T DO THIS. IT HURTS, IT HURTS!!***

Never have I felt this fragile, like I'd walked straight onto broken glass. The pain shot through my spine was intense and sharp, magnifying every tiny sensation. It was as if any wrong move could easily rip my foot from the ankle. I was frozen, immobile, and completely dependent on d to help hobble my body over to the couch. When I instinctively lifted my right leg up, I immediately knew I was in trouble...

> ***AHHH!! COME HOLD UP MY FOOT!! THIS ANGLE PRECISELY!!***

Like supporting a toddler's neck, d had to gently cradle my ankle with one hand and the sole of my foot with the other, propping it against the neck of the reading lamp while my Achilles heel rested on the arm of the couch. I needed to stop the fluid from rushing to the inflammation. d then brought me a *Waschhandschuh* stuffed with ice pouches to relieve the pain. For a few minutes, I lay there stiff, reverse-planking.

> *Oh no, I have arthritis. This must be it.*

How did Dad manage his pain? I began chugging liters of water to flush the poison out of my system—something about diluting the crystals in the blood, I recalled him saying. I called my parents.

> *“You’ll be fine. You just don't stay hydrated enough.”*

My gut told me something was off. It had started last Wednesday with a stiffness around my right ankle, which I’d dismissed as either crouching too much or running too aggressively after the triathlon. By the end of the week, the tenderness was hard to ignore, to the point where I’d begun propping it up underneath my desk at work to softly massage it.

> *Could it actually be a minor sprain?*

I instantly knew dinner was the culprit before even scrambling to confirm with G, or with Dad. What perplexed me however, was the fact that Dad was playing with the devil that evening, *the last meal*, by picking a restaurant that could’ve sent us both straight home in a taxi (we'd need a mini van with enough leg space). With every sip of Chardonnay paired with alternating bites of lobster and steak, the pain pricked up my nervous system until it hit my brain, sounding the alarm that something needed my attention.

I know Dad loves seafood, shellfish especially. Every autumn he begins craving crabs, driving the family out to seaports to find the freshest catch. He’s always more than willing to splurge on a special occasion, and that night was no exception. Life's too short to deprive ourselves of what we love most, I imagine him say. Dad never thinks twice about the things he sets his mind to—a principle he cemented when he was my age, reminding me of it every single year.

> *“I preordered two whole lobsters.”*

I eyed the $53 claws on the right and the $45 innocent stock on the left. In the center it read: **UNLIMITED FRIES**. This was going to cost me. And it absolutely did.
